Curve essentials: parameter, continuity, and local control
Key ideas to watch throughout the lecture
• Parametric form: move a point along the curve by
changing u: f(u)=(x,y)
• C0 means connected, C1 adds tangent
continuity, C2 adds curvature continuity.
• Geometric continuity (G1, G2) can match shape
even when parameter speed differs.
• Local control means one edit changes only a
nearby region; this is crucial in CAD.
Visual cue: a sharp corner usually means C0 but not C1.

Practice B — Weights, knots, and the jump to NURBS
What students do
• Display the weight circles around control points.
• Break the equality of the radii so weights can vary
independently.
• Increase one weight and observe the curve being
attracted to that control point. (select circle + D)
• Discuss why zero and negative weights are not
allowed in the usual workflow.
• Relate nonuniform knots or repeated knots to
interpolation behavior.
Observe and explain
A rational B-spline is a NURBS curve. The “R” comes
from the ratio introduced by the weights.

Classroom demo script

Use Draft Edit to move points until the profile is
clean and closed.
For a quick solid: switch to Part Workbench and

Practice E — Use Shape From Text for raised or engraved features
ShapeString workflow

What students should watch

1) Switch to Draft Workbench and launch Shape
From Text.
2) Set the text string, choose a font file, then
define size and placement.
3) Place the text on a plane or on the face that
will receive the feature.
4) For raised text: use PartDesign Pad or Part
Extrude.
5) For engraved text: use PartDesign Pocket to
cut the letters into the solid.

• Text behaves like geometry, not editable typography.
• The font file controls the letter outlines.
• Closed inner loops (A, O, P, R…) become holes in the final feature.
• Pocket cuts inward; Pad or Extrude adds material outward.

Recap
Quick recap
• Hermite: endpoints + tangents.
• Cardinal: interpolating cubic with tension and local
control.
• Bézier: endpoint interpolation, control polygon, de
Casteljau.
• B-spline: piecewise polynomial, local control, knots.
• NURBS: weighted rational extension of B-splines.
