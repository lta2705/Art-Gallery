Why This Topic Matters in Computer Graphics
• Global illumination adds believable energy
transfer: colored bounce light, corner darkening, and
soft ambient lift.
• Shadows create spatial relationships: contact,
scale, depth ordering, and time-of-day cues.
• The same scene may need different methods for
offline realism, real-time interaction, or limited
hardware.
• Good engineers choose the cheapest method that
still preserves the visual message of the design.

Direct Lighting vs. Global Illumination

• Direct lighting: paths go from a light source >>
surface >> the eye after one interaction.
• Indirect lighting: one or more additional
bounces from other surfaces before reaching
the eye.
• Global illumination is the combined solution:
direct + indirect transport across the full scene.

Light Transport Intuition
• Outgoing radiance =emitted light + reflected
incoming light.
• Reflected light depends on material response
(BRDF), visibility, angle, and distance.
• The full problem is global because any surface may
illuminate any other surface after enough bounces.
• Naively considering every interaction is expensive,
so rendering uses sampling, caching, or
precomputation.

Shadow Families: Hard, Soft, Image-Space, Geometry-Space
• Hard shadows: a light is treated as a point or when visibility is
binary.
• Soft shadows contain a penumbra because only part of an
area light is visible from a point.
• Image-space methods store visibility in textures or depth
maps; geometry-space methods build shadow regions
explicitly.
• Today we connect soft-shadow intuition to two practical
families: shadow mapping and shadow volumes.

From Particle Tracing to Photon Mapping
• Particle tracing sends light particles from
luminaires into the scene and records where they
land.
• This is useful for diffuse indirect lighting because
many surface texels can accumulate incoming
energy offline.
• Photon mapping extends the same idea by storing
photon hits in a spatial data structure for later
radiance estimates.
• The key design change is separating “trace and
store photons” from “query stored photons while
rendering the image.”

Shadow Mapping Idea
• Render the scene once from the light’s point of view and store
the nearest depth in a shadow map.
• During camera rendering, transform each fragment into light
space and compare its depth (relative to the light source) with
the stored value.
• If the fragment is farther than the stored depth it’s in shadow.
• The method is GPU-friendly because the main data structure
is a depth texture, not explicit shadow geometry.

Shadow Mapping Common Artifacts
• Shadow acne happens when numerical precision makes a lit fragment compare against
nearly equal stored depth. Use bias to solve this.
• Peter-panning happens when bias is too large and the shadow detaches from the caster.
• Aliasing (Jagged Edges): If the shadow map resolution is too low, the shadows look like
they are made of giant Lego bricks.

Shadow Volumes Idea
• Shadow volumes start from silhouette edges:
places where one face sees the light and the
adjacent face does not.
• Each silhouette edge is extruded away from the
light to create a closed volume of shadowed space.
• The stencil buffer counts whether the eye ray
enters and leaves that volume before hitting a visible
surface.
• This gives crisp, resolution-independent hard
shadows without storing a depth map.

Shadow Volumes: Strengths and Trade-Offs
• Strength: hard-shadow edges are exact with
respect to the shadow volume geometry, not
shadow-map texels.
• Strength: image resolution changes do not change
the geometric shadow boundary.
• Cost: silhouette extraction and volume rendering
become expensive for very complex or animated
meshes.
• Limitation: the classic approach is best for hard
shadows; soft shadows usually need extensions or
another method.

Key Takeaways
• Path tracing follows eye paths and is the cleanest
general GI model, but it needs enough samples to
control noise.
• Photon mapping reuses precomputed light
particles, which is especially helpful for indirect light
reuse and caustics.
• Shadow mapping is the default real-time
workhorse: easy to deploy, sensitive to depth
precision and map resolution.
• Shadow volumes trade shadow-map aliasing for
geometry and stencil work, producing sharp hard
shadows.
• As designers and engineers, you should now be
able to explain, choose, and prototype these
methods.
